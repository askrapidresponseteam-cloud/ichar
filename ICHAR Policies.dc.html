How We Handle Things

Plain-language statements of what we do with information you give us, how we try to keep this site usable, and the terms on which it is published.

01 · Privacy

What We Do With What You Tell Us

Information lodged with ICHAR stays within the system and is not shared outside it unless you authorise it, or a law or a court requires it. That applies to everything in a report: names, contacts, documents and photographs.

What we collect

What you enter in an application: the incident, its place and date, who is affected, and the evidence you say exists.
Your contact details, unless you file anonymously.
Correspondence with intake and the division handling the matter.
For contributions: name, email, amount, designation and, if you give it, PAN for a tax receipt.

Why we hold it

To assess whether a matter can be acted on, to prepare complaints, filings and representations, to keep an auditable record of what was done in your name, and - in anonymised, aggregated form - to compile thematic reports. We do not sell data, and we do not use it for advertising.

Anonymous reports

You may report without giving a name or contact. We can still record and examine the matter, but we cannot ask follow-up questions, cannot tell you what was filed, and in some proceedings cannot proceed without an identifiable complainant.

On this device

So that you can track an application, this site keeps your reference number and a copy of what you submitted in your own browser's storage. Clearing site data removes it from the device; it does not affect the council's record. Ask us if you want the council's copy corrected or erased.

Your choices

Ask what is held about you, and ask for it to be corrected.
Withdraw permission to share your details with an authority or partner.
Ask for erasure, except where a filed matter or statutory audit requires the record to stand.

Write to us with “Privacy” in the subject line and we will reply within a fortnight.

02 · Accessibility

Usable by the People Who Need It

People come to a rights body in distress, often on an old phone and a slow connection. We treat that as a design constraint, not an afterthought.

What we hold ourselves to

Text and controls that meet WCAG 2.1 AA contrast, and type that scales when you zoom.
Every action reachable by keyboard, with a visible focus outline.
Animation that stops when your device asks for reduced motion.
Touch targets no smaller than 44 pixels, and forms that work in one column on a phone.
Plain language, and legal citations explained rather than assumed.

Known gaps

The site is English-only. Hindi, Tamil and Bengali versions are planned; until then intake accepts applications in those languages by email.
Published reports are not yet tagged PDFs. Ask and we will send an accessible text version.
Secure document upload is not live; evidence is accepted by email in the meantime.

Blocked by something here? Tell us what happened and on what device - we treat it as a defect, not feedback.

03 · Terms

Terms of Use

No lawyer-client relationship

Material on this site is general information about rights and remedies, not legal advice on your facts. Sending an application, and our receiving it, does not by itself create a lawyer-client relationship. That begins only when ICHAR confirms in writing that it is acting in the matter.

Accuracy of legal references

Statutory references - including the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 and the rules cited across the divisions - are given as a guide and are current to the best of our knowledge at the time of writing. Law changes; verify before relying on a provision.

Emergencies

ICHAR is not an emergency service. Where a person or animal is in immediate danger, contact the statutory helpline - 112 for emergencies, 1098 for children, 181 for women - and then tell us.

Name, logo and content

The ICHAR name and logo may not be used without written consent. Reports and policy briefs may be quoted with attribution. Partner organisations use each other's marks only as an executed MoU permits.

Contributions

Contributions are applied to charitable objects under Section 8 of the Companies Act, 2013. Designations are honoured wherever practicable; where a designated programme is fully funded, the council may apply the amount to the nearest object and will say so. Tax benefits depend on registrations that are still pending.

Governing law

These terms are governed by the laws of India, with jurisdiction in the courts at New Delhi.
