Objective · Vision · Mission

Why the Council Exists

Objective

To uphold, protect and enforce the rights and dignity of humans and animals through law, advocacy, research and sustainable action at national and international levels.

To combat injustice, cruelty and exploitation affecting humans, animals and the environment through protection, prevention, legal action and public awareness.

Vision

A world governed by dignity, justice and compassion for all living beings.

A just, compassionate and sustainable world where the rights, dignity and well-being of humans, animals and the environment are universally respected and protected, enabling peaceful coexistence among all forms of life.

Mission

To defend rights, prevent injustice and drive systemic change through law, awareness and global cooperation.

Legal advocacy, research, education, policy engagement and international collaboration - ensuring justice, accountability and sustainable development for present and future generations.

What ICHAR Actually Does

A rights-protection, policy-intervention and institutional-accountability body operating at the intersection of law, governance, ethics and welfare.

A

Rights Protection & Legal Intervention

  • Legal aid facilitation and strategic litigation
  • Complaint drafting and representation before authorities
  • Monitoring compliance with national and international frameworks
  • Supporting victims through FIRs, PILs and representations

Focus · Systemic correction, not only individual relief

B

Documentation, Research & Reporting

  • Field investigations and case documentation
  • Thematic reports on women, children, animals and environment
  • Shadow reports for UN mechanisms and statutory bodies
  • Data-driven policy briefs and white papers

Focus · Credible records that compel action

C

Policy Advocacy & Institutional Reform

  • Ministries, statutory commissions and local administrations
  • Parliamentary committees and policy forums
  • International mechanisms, UN and treaty bodies
  • Memoranda, advisory opinions and draft amendments

Focus · Structural change over symbolic advocacy

D

Capacity Building & Education

  • Training police, officials, students, activists and community leaders
  • Rights literacy programmes for vulnerable populations
  • Workshops on animal welfare, child protection and environmental justice
  • Certified compliance training for institutions and CSR teams

Focus · Prevention through knowledge

E

Emergency Response & Rapid Action

  • Mass animal cruelty
  • Environmental damage in progress
  • Custodial abuse or institutional neglect
  • Legal response teams mobilised
  • Media documentation
  • Escalation to the competent authority
  • Coordination with on-ground partners

Focus · Speed, legality and accountability

Whom We Protect

Women

Survivors of domestic violence, sexual abuse, trafficking and workplace harassment; women denied access to justice, property or dignity; those failed by institutional apathy.

Legal basis · CEDAW · BNS 2023 · POCSO · Constitutional rights

Children

Victims of abuse, neglect, exploitation, child labour and trafficking; children in conflict with law or in institutional care; children affected by poverty, disaster or displacement.

Legal basis · CRC · JJ Act · POCSO Act

Animals

Street animals subjected to cruelty or poisoning; wildlife affected by illegal trade or habitat destruction; animals in captivity, experimentation, transport or slaughter.

Legal basis · PCA Act 1960 · Wildlife Protection Act 1972

Communities impacted by pollution, deforestation and illegal mining; coastal, tribal and rural populations facing ecological injustice; future generations whose rights are compromised.

Think · Act · Influence

How a Council Works

A council is not simply an implementing NGO. Its strength lies in policy influence, coordination, advocacy and standards - while still supporting selective ground action.

01

Policy Advocacy & Law Reform

Study existing law, identify gaps, misuse and non-implementation, then submit policy briefs, draft amendments and recommendations to governments, ministries, courts and UN bodies - including as an expert body or amicus where permitted.

02

Research & Reports

Violation reports, cruelty and environmental-crime documentation, state-wise status reports, an annual State of Rights report and thematic studies. The reports are the credibility engine.

03

Advisory & Capacity Building

Training for police, local authorities, NGOs and corporate CSR teams; workshops, legal literacy programmes and rights awareness campaigns. The council is a knowledge backbone, not an activist group.

04

Coordination & Accreditation

Act as an umbrella body: accredit grassroots organisations, set ethical and operational standards, convene collaboration between NGOs, governments and international agencies, and maintain databases of activists, lawyers and researchers.

05

Strategic Interventions

Not everything - high-impact cases only: strategic litigation, emergency response in extreme abuse cases, and pilot projects designed so partners can scale them.

06

International Representation

Participation in UN side events, global conferences and treaty consultations; MoUs with global councils and NGOs; transnational advocacy as the council expands beyond India.
