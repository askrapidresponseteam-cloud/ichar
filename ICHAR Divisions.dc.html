Division 01 · Women & Child Rights

Human
Rights

We document and legally challenge violations against women and children under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, ensuring dignity, safety and justice through rights-based enforcement.

What we act on

Sexual violence & bodily integrity
Rape, aggravated rape and gang rape · BNS §§63, 64, 70
Rape causing death or a permanent vegetative state · BNS §66
Non-penetrative sexual assault and harassment · BNS §§62, 75
Stalking, online and physical, and voyeurism · BNS §§78, 77
Domestic & intimate-partner violence
Cruelty by a husband or relatives · BNS §85
Dowry death · BNS §80
Abetment of suicide · BNS §108, with civil remedies under the PWDVA, 2005
Trafficking & forced practices
Human trafficking and trafficking of women · BNS §§143, 144
Forced prostitution and exploitative transactions · BNS §§145, 146
Acid attack and attempted acid attack · BNS §§124, 125
Honour killing and criminal intimidation · BNS §§101, 103, 351
Child rights
Rape and gang rape of a minor · BNS §§65, 70(2), with POCSO
Sexual exploitation and child sexual abuse material · BNS §§96, 95
Child trafficking, forced begging and sale of children · BNS §§143(2), 146, 146(2)
Abandonment, physical abuse and kidnapping · BNS §§93, 115–117, 137
Child marriage and forced early pregnancy · PCMA 2006 with BNS abetment offences

Allied statutes

POCSO Act, 2012 — child sexual offences
Juvenile Justice Act — care and protection
PWDVA, 2005 — civil remedies in domestic violence
PCMA, 2006 — child marriage
CEDAW and the CRC — international obligations

Constitutional linkage

Art. 14 · Equality Art. 15(3) · Protection Art. 21 · Life & dignity Art. 39(f) · Children

In case documentation we cite the BNS section first, then allied law, and record the human rights violation alongside the criminal offence.

Division 02 · Cruelty, Trade & Wildlife

Animal
Rights

We work to prevent cruelty, exploitation and illegal killing of animals by enforcing India's animal protection laws and the constitutional duty of compassion.

What we act on

Cruelty & violence
Beating, kicking, torturing, maiming, poisoning or burning animals
Starvation, abandonment and continuous tethering
Overloading draught animals and cruel use in labour
Killing animals in a cruel manner · PCA §11 with BNS §325
Community animals & illegal culling
Poisoning of street dogs or cattle, and mass culling without authority
Forced eviction or relocation of community animals
Non-compliance with the Animal Birth Control (Dogs) Rules, 2023
Trade, transport & slaughter
Illegal animal trade and wildlife or exotic species smuggling
Cruel transport in breach of the Transport of Animals Rules, 1978
Slaughter outside licensed premises and prohibited slaughter
Entertainment, testing & wildlife
Circus exploitation and organised animal fights
Cosmetic testing and unethical laboratory practice · PCA §14, CPCSEA
Poaching, captivity and habitat destruction
Mismanaged human–wildlife conflict

Governing law

PCA Act, 1960 §§11, 14 — cruelty, neglect, experiments
BNS, 2023 §325 — mischief by killing or maiming animals
Wildlife Protection Act, 1972 — hunting, trade, captivity
ABC (Dogs) Rules, 2023 · Pet Shop Rules, 2018
Performing Animals Rules, 2001 · Transport Rules, 1978
Customs Act, 1962 and CITES — cross-border trade

Constitutional & judicial

Art. 21 · Life Art. 48A · Wildlife Art. 51A(g) · Compassion

Animal Welfare Board v. A. Nagaraja (2014) and subsequent rulings are the backbone of our pleadings. Street dogs cannot be killed or relocated except as the law allows.

Division 03 · Pollution, Land & Climate

Environmental
Rights

We protect the right to a clean, safe and sustainable environment by enforcing India's environmental laws and the constitutional duties behind them.

What we act on

Air & water
Industrial emissions beyond limits and operation without control devices
Open burning of waste, stubble burning, construction dust
Untreated effluent and sewage discharge into rivers, lakes and sea
Groundwater contamination, oil spills and chemical leaks
Land, forest & biodiversity
Illegal mining and hazardous waste dumping on land
Encroachment of wetlands and forests, illegal felling
Forest land diversion without clearance
Coastal, mangrove and marine destruction in CRZ areas
Waste, industry & climate
Illegal landfills, plastic, e-waste and biomedical waste mishandling
Operating without environmental clearance or breaching consent conditions
False environmental compliance reporting and hazardous chemical leaks
Environmental justice
Forced displacement without rehabilitation
Denial of clean drinking water and health impacts of pollution
Disproportionate ecological burden on tribal, coastal and rural communities

Governing law

Environment (Protection) Act, 1986
Air Act, 1981 · Water Act, 1974
National Green Tribunal Act, 2010
Forest Conservation Act, 1980 · Biological Diversity Act, 2002
MMDR Act, 1957 · Hazardous Wastes Rules, 2016
CRZ Notification · EIA Notification, 2006
Public Liability Insurance Act, 1991 · LARR Act, 2013

Principles we plead

Precautionary principle Polluter pays Public trust doctrine Sustainable development

Complaints go to the State or Central Pollution Control Board first, then to the National Green Tribunal or the High Court as a PIL where the board does not act.

From your message to a filing

How a Case Moves

Every application passes through the same four stages, whichever division receives it. You are told which stage you are at, and what we can and cannot do next.

Stage 01

Intake & routing

Your application is logged with a reference number and routed to the division and the office nearest the incident. Urgent matters are flagged the same day.

Stage 02

Documentation

We verify what is verifiable — statements, photographs, medical or municipal records, site visits where needed — and identify the sections the facts support.

Stage 03

Legal action

FIR support, complaints to the competent authority, representations, NGT petitions or a PIL — with legal aid facilitation where the person affected cannot afford counsel.

Stage 04

Follow-through

Compliance monitoring, compensation tracking, and — where the failure is structural — the case feeds a thematic report or a policy submission.
