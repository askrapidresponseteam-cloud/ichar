Apex governance · head office, New Delhi

How the Council Is Governed

Governing council · board of directors

The supreme policy-making body, seven to eleven members: a chairperson, vice-chairperson, secretary-general, treasurer and four to seven independent directors.

Sets vision, mission and long-term strategy
Approves budgets, annual plans and policies
Ensures legal compliance — Section 8, FCRA, CSR-1, UN ECOSOC in time
Appoints the Executive Director and approves State Directors

Executive secretariat · Delhi

Headed by the Executive Director / Secretary-General, running day-to-day national and international operations through eight departments.

01 · Human Rights Division — Women's Rights, Child Rights
02 · Animal Rights Division
03 · Environmental Rights Division
04 · Legal & Policy Affairs — litigation support, research, UN liaison
05 · International Relations & Partnerships
06 · Finance, Audit & Compliance
07 · Administration & HR
08 · Communications & Public Affairs — media, reports, campaigns

Branch offices · Chennai, Mumbai, Kolkata

Each branch is administratively independent and policy-controlled by Delhi. A State / Regional Director reports directly to the Executive Director.

Deputy Director, Programs Legal Officer Finance & Admin Officer Outreach & Field Coordinator

Reporting & control

Vertical
Branch units → State Director → Executive Director, Delhi → Governing Council
Horizontal
Branch legal units ↔ HQ Legal & Policy Division · Branch programme units ↔ HQ programme heads

Every case carries a reference from intake to closure, so a file can be located at any level of the structure.

Advisory & oversight bodies

Who Checks the Council

Non-executive bodies that keep the council credible with courts, donors, statutory regulators and UN mechanisms.

National Advisory Council

A non-executive body of retired judges, former civil servants, international experts and UN consultants. It advises on strategy and lends independent scrutiny to major submissions.
